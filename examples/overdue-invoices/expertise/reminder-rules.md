# How Mara decides (her words, written down in the first meeting)

> Fictitious example. This is the expertise step: the person who does the
> work writes down how she decides, in her own words. Nothing here is
> technical. The agent's rules and tests are built from this page.

1. I only look at invoices that are not paid.
2. **First reminder** when an invoice is 10 days or more past its due date.
   Friendly, short, "perhaps it was overlooked", with the invoice number,
   amount and due date.
3. **Second reminder** at 20 days or more. Still polite, but clear: please
   pay within 10 days or tell us if something is wrong.
4. At **35 days or more** I do not write a third email. I give the case to
   our managing director, who calls the customer.
5. **Never** remind a customer with an open complaint. I ask the colleague
   who handles the complaint first.
6. **Never** remind a customer who has a payment plan with us.
7. Invoices **under CHF 50** I leave until the end of the year; a reminder
   costs more than it brings.
8. The emails are in German, formal ("Sie"), signed with my name, and never
   mention fees or legal steps; that decision stays with the managing
   director.
9. When I am unsure, I ask. A wrong reminder to a good customer costs more
   than a late one.

## Open questions for Mara (from the reviews, 20.10.)

1. **Small invoice with a complaint or a payment plan.** Today the tool puts
   an invoice under CHF 50 on hold, even when the customer has a complaint
   open. Nothing is sent, but nobody is asked either. Should the complaint
   win, so the case goes to a person?
2. **Two rules the tool added because of your rule 9.** A zero or negative
   amount (a credit note?) and an invoice without an email address go to a
   person instead of getting a draft. Do you agree?
3. **The wording of the second reminder** (below).

### The second reminder

The second reminder says "trotz unserer Erinnerung" (despite our reminder).
In the first week of using the tool, an invoice can already be 20 or more
days late without a first reminder ever having been sent. Should the second
reminder then use the first reminder's text, or should the tool look up
whether a first reminder went out? Until Mara answers, the text stays as she
wrote it and she checks it before sending.
